# Judging Criteria — 2026–27

HackIndia events can use different scoring rubrics. If your event publishes an official rubric, that rubric takes priority.

The following framework is a strong preparation baseline.

| Category | What judges look for |
|---|---|
| **Problem & Impact** | Is the problem real, important, and clearly understood? |
| **Innovation** | Is the approach original or meaningfully better than existing solutions? |
| **Technical Execution** | Does the prototype work? Is the architecture sensible and technically strong? |
| **Technology Use** | Is AI, Web3, cybersecurity, IoT, or other technology used meaningfully? |
| **User Experience** | Is the product usable, understandable, and well designed? |
| **Scalability** | Could the solution grow beyond the hackathon prototype? |
| **Demo & Presentation** | Can the team explain and demonstrate the solution clearly? |

## How to Score Better

### Show, don't just tell
A working demo is more convincing than a long slide deck.

### Explain your technical decisions
Be ready to answer:
- Why this technology?
- Why this architecture?
- What happens when the system scales?
- What are the limitations?
- How did you test it?

### Quantify impact
Use measurable results where possible:
- Time saved
- Cost reduced
- Accuracy
- Latency
- Detection rate
- Users served
- Energy saved
- Transactions processed

### Be honest about limitations
Judges value a team that understands what is complete, what is experimental, and what comes next.

## Pitch Structure

**Problem → Users → Solution → Demo → Technology → Innovation → Impact → Future**

> Your event may specify a different presentation time. Always follow the official event instructions.
