# Team Guidelines

To ensure smooth presentations and a professional hackathon experience, please carefully follow these guidelines.

## Technical & Presentation Preparation

- Ensure that your **project files and presentation** are available with **all team members** as a backup.
  - If one laptop fails, another teammate should have everything ready.
- The laptop you use on stage must have an **HDMI port**.
  - Avoid relying on Type-C adapters or MacBooks, as they may cause last-minute connectivity issues.
- **Practice your pitch as a team** before the finals:
  - Do at least **2–3 mock runs** with a timer (**4 min pitch + 4 min Q&A**).
  - Simulate the stage setup, including switching between slides and the demo.
  - Rotate who answers different types of judge questions to build confidence.
- Keep introductions short.
  - A simple **“We are Team [Name], from [College/City]”** is enough.
  - Focus your time on the project, not lengthy introductions.
- Judges expect to see a **working demo**.
  - Practice smooth transitions between slides and demo.
  - Make sure your demo can run offline in case of internet issues.

## 💻 Demo & Backup Checklist

Before your presentation:

- [ ] Project runs successfully
- [ ] Presentation opens correctly
- [ ] Demo data is ready
- [ ] Hardware is powered and tested
- [ ] Required cables/adapters are available
- [ ] HDMI output has been tested
- [ ] Internet/API dependencies have a backup
- [ ] Offline demo or recorded demo is available
- [ ] GitHub repository is accessible
- [ ] README is complete
- [ ] No passwords, API keys, or private credentials are exposed

## Interaction with Judges

- Be **respectful and professional** when answering questions.
- Judges want to understand your project better — treat questions as an opportunity to showcase your work, not as a grilling session.
- If you don't know an answer, it is okay to say so. **Honesty is valued more than making something up.**
- Listen to the complete question before answering.
- Keep answers concise and directly related to the project.
- If another teammate is better suited to answer, let them respond.

## 🤝 Code of Conduct

- Be kind and respectful to other teams, mentors, volunteers, judges, and organizers.
- Do not interrupt or deliberately disrupt another team's presentation.
- Build connections — the hackathon is also about networking and collaboration.
- Celebrate everyone's effort. Winning is important, but learning and sharing are also part of the experience.
- Follow venue, event, safety, and organizer instructions at all times.

## 🎯 Final Presentation Strategy

Remember:

**Problem → Solution → Innovation → Demo → Technology → Impact → Future**

Keep the presentation focused on what you built and why it matters.

### Avoid

- Long team introductions
- Reading every word from slides
- Unnecessary technical jargon
- Claims that you cannot demonstrate
- Last-minute code changes
- Exposing passwords or API keys
- Depending entirely on one laptop or internet connection

## Final Note

Relax, don't take too much pressure, and enjoy the experience.

**This is your moment — live it fully, present with confidence, and have fun! 🚀**

> **Build. Innovate. Compete. Launch.**
